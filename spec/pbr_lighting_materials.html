Human explainer · RTXDI + RTXGI + OMM + DLSS-RR

PBR, lights, and the RTX frame

Part I builds the shading of a single surface point from glTF data, one symbol at a time. Every graph and scene on this page evaluates the same formulas the engine runs. Part II shows where RTXDI, RTXGI NRC, opacity micromaps and DLSS-RR sit in the frame, and what each one reads and writes. The engine targets NVIDIA RTX GPUs only.

New to PBRStart at §1. Each section opens with a one-line summary. Know the mathJump to the live material and light lab, then Part II. ImplementingStart at the frame graph in §11, then read the plan (top bar).

Part I

Shade one point

Inputs: a surface point, the direction to the camera, a light. Output: how much light leaves toward the camera.

1

The one equation

In one lineOutgoing light = self-glow + for each light (how the surface redirects it × how bright it arrives × how squarely it hits).

Lo = emissive + Σlights f(V, L) · Li · NdotL
SymbolWhat it isPlain
Nunit surface normal, after the normal map and the double-sided flipwhich way the surface faces
Vunit vector to the camerawhere you look from
Lunit vector to the lightwhere the light comes from
Hnormalize(L + V)the tilt a tiny mirror needs to bounce L into V
fBRDF, units 1/srthe share of light from L that leaves toward V
Lilight arriving at this pointhow bright the lamp is here
NdotLmax(N·L, 0)a grazing beam spreads over more area

The engine evaluates f in ray-traced passes (material.brdf). There is one implementation, and every pass calls it.

2

Metal vs dielectric

In one lineMetals have no diffuse and colored reflections. Everything else has colored diffuse and a 4 % white reflection.

Dielectric

Plastic, wood, paint, stone. Light mostly enters, scatters among pigments, and comes back out colored.

baseColor is albedo. Head-on, 4 % reflects as white (f0 = 0.04, from index of refraction 1.5).

Metal

Gold, iron, chrome. Light does not enter, so there is no diffuse at all. The color is the reflection.

baseColor is f0. Gold ≈ (1.00, 0.77, 0.34), chrome ≈ (0.55, 0.55, 0.55).

glTF blends the two with one scalar, metallic. The blend collapses into three shader inputs:

c_diff = baseColor · (1 − metallic)           // metals: no diffuse
f0     = lerp(0.04, baseColor, metallic)      // metals: colored reflection
α      = roughness²                           // GGX uses α, not roughness
Spec noteCurrent Appendix B does not scale dielectric albedo by an extra (1 − 0.04). Energy balance comes from the (1 − F) on the diffuse term (§7).

Worked sRGB example and packing traps

Texel [64, 124, 231, 255] with factor [0.2, 1.0, 0.7, 1.0] decodes to linear [0.051, 0.202, 0.799, 1], so baseColor ≈ [0.010, 0.202, 0.559, 1]. Lighting sRGB values directly gives grey metals and neon plastics.

Authors often put AO in the unused R of the metal-rough image and reference that image twice. Core glTF never reads R on the metal-rough slot. The engine stores UVs as v′ = 1 − v for Vulkan.

4

Fresnel: why edges go white

In one lineEvery material becomes a mirror at grazing angles. f0 sets only the head-on value.

F(f0, VdotH) = f0 + (1 − f0) · (1 − VdotH)5

At VdotH = 0.5 a dielectric reflects 7 %, still mostly diffuse. At 0.2 it reflects 35 %. That is the wet sheen at a glancing view.

6

Specular: D and V

In one lineD counts facets aimed at H. V removes the ones hidden from light or camera.

a2 = α²
D  = a2 / (π · (NdotH² · (a2 − 1) + 1)²)                         // peak 1/(π α²) at H = N
V  = 0.5 / (NdotL·√(NdotV²(1−a2)+a2) + NdotV·√(NdotL²(1−a2)+a2))  // height-correlated Smith
f_specular = F · D · V
The two NdotLsThis V already contains the BRDF’s 1/(4·NdotL·NdotV). The light loop still multiplies by the rendering-equation NdotL, once. Drop it and grazing lights are too bright. Double it and they are too dark.
Separable Smith form (also spec-legal) 
G1(x) = 2x / (x + √(α² + (1 − α²)x²))
f_specular = F · D · G1(NdotL) · G1(NdotV) / (4 · NdotL · NdotV)

Any D/G pair is allowed. The inputs c_diff, f0, α must match Appendix B.

7

Diffuse + the full BRDF

In one lineDiffuse is c_diff/π, scaled by whatever Fresnel did not reflect.

f = (1 − F) · c_diff / π  +  F · D · V
Why 1/πA white Lambert surface under uniform light must reflect exactly what it receives. Integrating cos θ over the hemisphere gives π, so f = 1/π. (1 − F) is the energy the specular lobe did not take.

9

Normal, AO, emissive, alpha

In one lineNone of these is a BRDF lobe. They change N, scale indirect light, add glow, or cut holes.

Normal map

n  = sample_linear(normalTex).xyz · 2 − 1
n  = normalize(float3(n.xy · scale, n.z))
B  = cross(N, T) · tangent.w
N  = normalize(T·n.x + B·n.y + N·n.z)

OpenGL +Y. Never flip Y. A file without tangents gets MikkTSpace-compatible ones generated at load, so a normal map always has a tangent frame.

Occlusion (AO)

ao scales indirect light only (RTXGI in §15). The sun still reaches a crack that faces it.

In a lights-only view, a correct AO map looks ignored.

Emissive

Added after lighting. It is not scaled by NdotL or AO. Factor ≤ 1 in core. HDR needs KHR_materials_emissive_strength.

Alpha

OPAQUE ignores a. MASK cuts at alphaCutoff (0.5 by default). BLEND is straight-alpha over after lighting. Never premultiply before the BRDF.

In the engine, the G-buffer pass cuts MASK exactly with discard. Shadow and bounce rays use opacity micromaps (§13).

doubleSided: no culling, and N = −N on back faces before the BRDF.

10

Punctual lights

In one lineThe three light types only change how L and Li are built. The BRDF is the same.

Li = color · intensity · distanceAttenuation · coneAttenuation
TypeLintensityAttenuation
directional−worldDir (node −Z)lux1
pointnormalize(lightPos − P)candelawindow(d) / d²
spotsame as pointcandelapoint × cone

Distance: inverse square with a range window

attenuation = window(d) / max(d², 1e-4)
window(d)   = range == 0 ? 1 : saturate(1 − (d / range)⁴)     // exactly 0 at d ≥ range

Spot cone

cd     = dot(spotDir, −L)                        // +1 on the axis
scale  = 1 / max(0.001, cos(inner) − cos(outer))
offset = −cos(outer) · scale
cone   = saturate(cd · scale + offset)²
SignL points toward the light and spotDir points away from it, so on-axis dot(spotDir, −L) = +1. Writing dot(spotDir, L) turns the hot spot black and lights a ring.

The loop

Lo = emissive
for each node-attached light:
    (L, Li) = evalLight(light, P)
    if NdotL ≤ 0: continue
    Lo += evalBrdf(V, L) · Li · NdotL · visible(P, L)
In this engineThis loop defines the answer, but the engine never runs it. With hundreds of lights, looping over all of them per pixel is too slow, so RTXDI (§14) picks a few lights per pixel and estimates the same sum.

Exposure belongs to the engine. A 1 cd point light at 1 m on white Lambert gives 1/π ≈ 0.32 nits, far below SDR white.

Part II

Render the frame

Part I shaded one point. A frame needs about 8 million of them, plus shadows, bounced light, and a clean image from very few rays. Four NVIDIA SDKs do that work, and the engine only writes the glue each one asks for.

11

The frame at a glance

In one lineRaster the first hit into a G-buffer, then RTXDI adds direct light, RTXGI NRC adds bounced light, and DLSS-RR denoises and upscales.

ConcernOwnerThe engine writes
direct lightRTXDI (ReSTIR DI)RAB_* bridge, light buffer, two compute entry points

One BRDF everywhereRTXDI’s target pdf, the NRC path loop and the G-buffer’s specular-albedo guide all call the evalBrdf from §7. When they disagree, it is a bug. There is no reference light loop, homegrown cache, or custom denoiser to compare against, and no vendor fallback path.

What the SDKs read: G-buffer and TLAS

In one lineThe rasterizer writes the first-hit surface once. Rays use standard KHR acceleration structures built from the same vertex and index buffers.

RTXDI, NRC and DLSS-RR all start from the surface the camera sees. The engine already draws meshes with mesh shaders and device-generated commands, so that pass writes a G-buffer: the BRDF inputs of §3 per pixel, not a lit color. Alpha testing there is an exact discard at the cutoff.

The engine keeps two G-buffers and swaps them every frame. RTXDI’s temporal reuse reads last frame’s surface to check that a reused light sample still belongs to a similar surface.

EventBLASTLASRTXDI light buffer
load an assetbuild one per primitive (with its OMM), grouped per modelnew instance rowsnew light rows

In this engineEvery loaded model owns a slice of each vertex, index and material buffer, and unloading a model moves the slices after it. So no ray-tracing data stores a fixed id. Each frame the CPU writes, next to every TLAS instance, a GpuRayInstance row with the current entity and primitive. At a ray hit, CommittedInstanceIndex() picks that row, which leads to the model’s slices and the primitive’s material, and CommittedPrimitiveIndex() gives the triangle. Three indices from the model’s index slice then give the vertices to interpolate. The same sampleMaterial runs at the hit as in the G-buffer fragment shader.
Why the first hit is rasterized, not traced

A camera ray and a rasterized pixel find the same surface. Raster already exists here, gives exact alpha edges with discard, and needs no ray-tracing pipeline or shader table. Every remaining ray (shadow, bounce) is a ray query in a compute shader.

DLSS needs the raster to be jittered by a sub-pixel offset each frame. Motion vectors are computed from the unjittered positions, and DLSS receives the jitter separately.

13

Opacity micromaps

In one linePre-classify tiny pieces of alpha-tested triangles, so shadow and bounce rays pass through leaf holes without running any shader.

A leaf card is two triangles with an alpha texture. Without help, a ray touching the card has to stop, fetch UV, sample alpha and compare to the cutoff. An opacity micromap splits each triangle into 4level micro-triangles, each marked opaque, transparent, or unknown. Traversal handles opaque and transparent in hardware.

RayAlpha handled byWhy
camera (first hit)raster discard in the G-buffer passexact edges where you look at them
shadow, bounceOMM, forced 2-state per instanceno shader runs during traversal. A slightly wrong edge is invisible in a soft shadow
In this engineThe OMM SDK bakes 4-state micromaps on the CPU at load time (with a disk cache), in index-buffer triangle order. Each micromap attaches to its primitive’s BLAS geometry. The instance flag FORCE_OPACITY_MICROMAP_2_STATE folds unknown into opaque or transparent at trace time. Instances without a micromap are FORCE_OPAQUE, so a ray query never sees a candidate it would have to alpha-test. The “any-hit calls” stat shows what 4-state tracing would cost.
14

RTXDI: direct light

In one lineTry a few random lights, keep the most promising one, and borrow good picks from neighbors and from last frame.

The §10 loop costs one BRDF evaluation and one shadow ray per light per pixel. A scene with 300 lamps makes that 300 rays per pixel. Picking one light at random is cheap but very noisy. RTXDI (ReSTIR DI) sits between the two:

  1. RIS: draw M candidate lights, weight each by its unshadowed contribution p̂ = luminance(f · Li · NdotL), and keep one in a reservoir.
  2. Temporal reuse: merge with this pixel’s reservoir from the previous frame.
  3. Spatial reuse: merge with a few neighbors’ reservoirs.
  4. Trace one shadow ray to the survivor and shade it with the reservoir weight.
The reservoir in five lines 
for each candidate xj (source pdf pj):
    wj = p̂(xj) / pj ;  wSum += wj ;  keep xj with probability wj / wSum
W   = wSum / (M · p̂(y))                     // unbiased contribution weight
Lo ≈ f(y) · Li(y) · NdotL · visible(y) · W

The demo has no occluders and merges reservoirs with the simple 1/M weights from the original paper. RTXDI adds pairwise MIS, visibility reuse, and a boiling filter.

Where RTXDI sits in the frame

RTXDI runs as two compute passes right after the G-buffer, before anything else lights the frame. Its only output is directRadiance, which noisy_color later adds to the indirect light.

PassWhat happensRays
rtxdi_initialdraw candidates from local lights (point, spot), infinite lights (directional) and one BRDF direction. Combine them into one reservoir per pixel and store itnone (unshadowed)
rtxdi_shademerge with last frame’s reservoir at the reprojected pixel and with a few neighbors (fused spatiotemporal), then trace a shadow ray to the survivor and write evalBrdf · Li · NdotL · W1 shadow ray per pixel

Who supplies what

RTXDI SDK

HLSL sampling and resampling (RTXDI_*), reservoir packing, neighbor-offset table, and the host ReSTIRDIContext that sizes buffers and fills the per-frame constants (SetFrameIndex, resampling parameters).

Engine (lighting/rtxdi_bridge.slang)

The RAB_* functions: read a surface from the G-buffer, sample a light with evalLight, compute p̂ with evalBrdf, sample and evaluate the BRDF pdf with sampleBrdf / pdfBrdf, and test visibility with a TLAS ray query.

In this engineThe CPU writes the light buffer every frame: point and spot lights first, then directional lights, each moved by its node’s world matrix. That fixed order lets RAB_TranslateLightIndex return its input unchanged. To check correctness, set RTXDI’s resampling mode to None with many initial samples (the SDK’s own unbiased setup) and compare against the default fused mode.
15

RTXGI NRC: bounced light

In one lineTrace a short path per pixel, then ask a small network, trained on this scene every frame, for the light that the rest of the path would have found.

Indirect light is the light arriving from other surfaces. Tracing it fully means long, noisy paths. The Neural Radiance Cache (NRC) learns “light leaving this point in this direction” for the current scene. Every pixel traces a short path and stops where the cache can take over. A sparse set of longer training paths teaches the cache what the scene really looks like, every frame, so it follows moving lights and objects.

Where NRC sits in the frame

NRC runs after RTXDI. The engine writes the two path passes and one combine pass; training, inference and the final write are host calls into the NRC SDK, recorded in the same command buffer. All paths start from the G-buffer surface, so no camera ray is traced again.

The SDK names above follow the NRC headers as described in the plan; check them against the pinned SDK version when it is vendored.

Why train every frameThe network starts empty and only knows what the training paths showed it recently. Too few training paths, or none while the camera moves, gives indirect light that lags behind the scene. The first surface never queries NRC for direct light, because RTXDI already handles it there.
In this engineThe NRC SDK allocates its own buffers; gi/nrc_bridge.slang binds them and must match the SDK’s HLSL buffer packing under Slang. The host library ships as prebuilt DLLs with CUDA runtime dependencies, which is acceptable because the engine targets NVIDIA RTX GPUs only. SHaRC, RTXGI’s hash-grid cache, is not used.

DLSS Ray Reconstruction

In one lineHand the denoiser a noisy image plus clean hints about the surfaces, and it rebuilds a sharp, stable frame at display resolution.

The lit color is noisy at 1 sample per pixel and rendered below display resolution. DLSS-RR replaces hand-written denoisers and upscales too. It needs guide buffers: noise-free descriptions of what is at each pixel. The G-buffer pass (§12) already writes all of them.

In this engineNGX runs on the host after noisy_color. It picks the render resolution for the chosen quality mode, and the G-buffer and all lighting passes run at that size. A GPU without Ray Reconstruction is an initialization error. There is no fallback denoiser.

module brdf;
import types;
import core.math;

// glTF appendix B; caller multiplies by radiance · NdotL once
public float3 evalBrdf(float3 n, float3 cDiff, float3 f0, float roughness, float3 v, float3 l)
{
    float nDotL = dot(n, l);
    if (nDotL <= 0.0) return 0.0;
    float3 h  = normalize(l + v);
    float  a2 = pow(max(roughness * roughness, 1e-4), 2.0);
    float3 f  = fresnelSchlick(f0, saturate(dot(v, h)));
    return (1.0 - f) * cDiff / kPi
         + f * ggxD(saturate(dot(n, h)), a2) * smithVisibility(nDotL, max(dot(n, v), 1e-4), a2);
}

The signature takes cDiff and f0 so the RTXDI bridge and the NRC path loop can call it straight from G-buffer data. The full contracts, the task order and the toolchain caveats are in pbr_implementation_plan.md.

When something looks wrong

18

Pitfalls

  1. Neon plastic, grey metalMetal-rough or normal maps sampled as sRGB.
  2. Default mesh nearly blackglTF default is rough metal. Correct without indirect light.
  3. Spot hot spot black, ring litdot(spotDir, L) instead of −L.
  4. Crevices black in sunlightAO applied to direct light.
  5. Highlight sharp until roughness ~0.7Forgot α = roughness².
  6. Grazing lights too bright or too darkMissing or doubled NdotL.
  7. Dents look like bumpsDirectX-style normal map Y.
  8. A light that never shows upLight definition with no node referencing it.
  9. Object moves, its shadow does notTLAS row not rewritten, or a light not following its entity.
  10. Shadows pop when the camera turnsCamera culling applied to TLAS instances.
  11. Leaves cast solid shadowsMicromap not attached to the BLAS, or the instance left FORCE_OPAQUE.
  12. RTXDI with reuse differs from mode NoneTarget pdf not built from the same evalBrdf/evalLight, or the previous G-buffer not swapped.
  13. Lights smear when the camera movesMotion vectors include the jitter, or point the wrong way (must be current → previous).
  14. Indirect light lags or smears after a changeNRC training paths missing or too few, or AO applied after Resolve instead of as the query path’s starting throughput.
  15. Smeared metals after DLSS-RRα written to the roughness guide, or lighting baked into albedo.
19

Glossary

BRDF
function of two directions giving the share of light redirected from one to the other (1/sr)
G-buffer
per-pixel BRDF inputs (normal, roughness, albedo, f0, depth, motion) written by the raster pass
BLAS / TLAS
bottom-level (one per glTF primitive) and top-level (instances with transforms) acceleration structures
ray query
tracing a ray inline from a compute shader, with no ray-tracing pipeline or shader table
OMM
opacity micromap: per-micro-triangle opaque, transparent or unknown state for alpha testing in traversal
DGC
device-generated commands: the raster path’s mesh-draw lists
RAB
RTXDI application bridge: the RAB_* functions the engine implements for the RTXDI headers
RIS
resampled importance sampling: draw cheap candidates, keep one in proportion to a better target
ReSTIR
RIS plus reuse of reservoirs across neighbors and frames. RTXDI implements ReSTIR DI
NRC
Neural Radiance Cache (RTXGI): a small network trained every frame that predicts the light leaving a surface point
DLSS-RR
DLSS Ray Reconstruction: learned denoise + upscale driven by guide buffers
Formulas follow Khronos glTF 2.0 Appendix B and KHR_lights_punctual, and match gltf_parse_resources.md §14. Every graph and scene evaluates the same F, D, V, attenuation, and cone math as material.brdf / lighting.lights. Agents: pbr_lighting_materials.md and pbr_implementation_plan.md.
